# Resume and cover letter assessment: Luca Kelly

Date: 2026-09-25
Documents reviewed:
- documents/Luca Kelly Electrician Resume.pdf
- documents/Luca Electrical Apprenticeship Cover Letter.pdf

Perspective: a small-to-medium Gold Coast electrical contractor hiring a first-year apprentice. The reviewer (main agent) wrote this as a judgement, not researched fact. Points that rely on research are referenced to research/02. Points not verified are marked.

---

## Verdict in one line

As an employer I would ring him. He already clears most of the hurdles that screen out first-year applicants. The documents undersell him in three ways: vague detail on his electrical experience, no education section with grades, and no start date.

## Strengths (keep these)

1. **The top banner works.** In one line an employer sees Cert II, White Card, P licence and own car, and currently employed. Those are exactly the screens in research/02 section 4. Keep it.
2. **Cert II in Electrotechnology completed.** Research/02 found some employers require it and others see it as strengthening an application. Many 17-year-old applicants won't have it.
3. **P licence (manual) and own car.** For a contractor who moves between sites, this is often the difference between hiring and not.
4. **Electrical work experience with a real electrician.** It shows he has seen the job and still wants it.
5. **Holds a job while at school.** It shows reliability, and employers read it that way.
6. **A practical project (speaker dock with switch).** It is something concrete to talk about at an interview.
7. **Tone of the cover letter.** "I am not expecting to walk in knowing everything" and the offer of a supervised trial are exactly right for a first-year apprentice. It reads as genuine, not over-polished.
8. **Clean, one-page layout.** Easy to scan, consistent headings, no clutter.

## Weaknesses (as an employer would see them)

### High impact

1. **No start date or availability.** He is a Year 11 student "looking for the opportunity to leave school". The employer immediately wonders whether he can start now, in December or next year. Unclear availability is an easy reason to put a resume aside.
2. **No Education section.** The resume never names his school (Varsity College appears only in the cover letter), his year level in context, or his subjects. "Strong school results, including Maths and Science" is a claim with no evidence. Maths is the most consistently reported screen for apprentices (research/02 section 4), so this is the most important claim to prove.
3. **Electrical work experience is vague.**
   - The electrician is unnamed ("Retiring Electrician").
   - There is no length: one day or three weeks?
   - The tasks are generic ("assisted with basic tasks, observed electrical installation work").
   - The Gold Coast electrical trade is a small network. A named business and specific tasks are far more credible, and an employer may know the person.
4. **The most relevant referee is missing.** The electrician he worked with is the reference an electrical employer most wants to hear from, and it isn't listed. The two listed referees have no contact details, so the employer has to make an extra request.

### Medium impact

5. **Missing dates.**
   - RTD Fabrication shows "Casual" where a date should be.
   - KFC shows "2026 – Present" with no start month or hours per week.
   - The Cert II has no completion date and no course code.
6. **The Skills & Attributes section is all self-description.** "Reliable, punctual, hard-working, learns quickly" appears on almost every apprentice resume, so employers skim past it. It also repeats what the work bullets already say. Evidence beats adjectives: "18 months at KFC, no missed shifts" says "reliable" better than the word does. (Only use figures that are true.)
7. **Bullets are generic and start with "I".** Several bullets under KFC and RTD could apply to any job. Australian resumes usually drop the "I" and start bullets with a verb ("Prepared tools and materials…").
8. **The Cert II section reads like the course outline.** Five of the six bullets describe the syllabus. Keep two or three, and lead with what he actually did.

### Cover letter specifically

9. **Generic greeting.** "Dear Hiring Manager" with no business named. Small contractors respond much better to a letter that names them and says why he chose them, e.g. the kind of work they do or where they operate. This takes two minutes per application.
10. **Weak first sentence.** "My name is Luca Kelly" wastes the opening; his name is already at the top. Open with what he offers: Cert II done, licence and car, available from [date].
11. **The KFC paragraph is longer than the electrical experience.** The weighting is backwards for an electrical employer. Swap it: expand the electrician work experience, and cut KFC to one sentence.
12. **No follow-up.** "I hope to hear from you" leaves it to the employer. Proactive is better: "I'll give you a call next week to see if a quick chat would suit."
13. **Small errors.** The last sentence is missing its full stop. The contact band repeats the header.

## Suggested improvements, in priority order

1. **Add an availability line** in the banner or profile. For example: "Available to start full-time from [date]." Decide the date as a family first.
2. **Add an Education section** under the profile. Include Varsity College, Year 11 (2026), relevant subjects and actual results for Maths and Science (e.g. grade or level). If the results are strong, show them. If they are average, remove "strong school results" rather than claim it.
3. **Rewrite the electrical work experience.** Name the electrician or business (with their permission), give the length (e.g. "5 days, June 2026"), and list 3 specific tasks he actually did. Examples of the kind of detail meant, only if true: pulled cable through a roof space, ran conduit, drilled and mounted boxes, helped fit off power points under supervision, did site clean-up and tool maintenance.
4. **Add the electrician as a referee.** Include phone numbers for all referees, with each referee's permission, instead of "available on request".
5. **Fill in all dates**: RTD Fabrication (month/year to month/year), KFC start month and typical hours, and the Cert II completion month and code (UEE22020 or UEE22025).
6. **Replace Skills & Attributes with a short "Why employ me" list of evidence**, or cut it to 4 items and move the space to the electrical experience.
7. **Tailor each cover letter**: the business name, one sentence on why them, and a follow-up line.
8. **Consider adding low-voltage rescue (LVR) and CPR certificates.** Research did not cover these. It is LIKELY (unverified) that Qld electrical employers need their workers to hold current LVR and CPR training. Having them already makes him cheaper and faster to put on site. It is a short course. Verify the Qld requirement and cost before doing it.
9. **Optional: one line on family support** for leaving school to start an apprenticeship. It pre-empts a question some employers have about a Year 11 student leaving early. Use only if the family is comfortable with it.
10. **Optional: a photo of the speaker dock project** to bring to interviews, not on the resume.
